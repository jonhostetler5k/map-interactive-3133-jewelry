## **The ProfitPath® Methodology Explained**

Most marketing agencies will tell you to "do everything." Run Google Ads, Meta Ads, TikTok, influencer campaigns, SEO, email marketing, and more—all at once. They'll promise that more channels equal more results.

That's a lie. More channels equal more confusion, diluted budgets, and no clear signal about what's actually working.

The ProfitPath® methodology is built on a different principle: **focus beats fragmentation.**

Instead of spreading your budget thin across every possible channel and audience, we concentrate resources on proving one path to profitability first. Once that path is proven, we scale it. Then, and only then, do we add a second path.

This approach has three critical advantages:

**1. Clarity Over Noise**

When you're running campaigns to three different audiences across five different platforms, you have no idea what's working. Is the low conversion rate due to the wrong audience, the wrong message, the wrong platform, or the wrong offer? You're drowning in data but starving for insight.

By focusing on one prospect and one set of campaigns, you get clean data. You know exactly what's working and what's not. This allows you to optimize rapidly instead of guessing.

**2. Capital Efficiency**

Marketing budgets are finite. Spreading $10,000 across ten campaigns means each campaign gets $1,000—not enough to generate statistically significant results. Concentrating $10,000 on one prospect and three campaigns means each campaign gets meaningful budget to test, learn, and scale.

**3. Speed to Proof**

The goal of Phase 1 isn't to "do marketing." It's to prove that you can acquire customers profitably. The faster you get to proof, the faster you can scale. Focus accelerates proof. Fragmentation delays it.

### **The Three-Phase Approach to Profitable Growth**

The ProfitPath® methodology follows a predictable, three-phase structure:

**Phase 1: Prove One Path (Months 1-3)**

Pick one prospect. Launch three campaigns targeting that prospect. Measure everything. The goal is proof: Can we acquire customers profitably? What's the cost per acquisition? What's the lifetime value? What messages resonate?

At the end of 90 days, you'll have clear answers. You'll know if this prospect is viable, what your unit economics look like, and whether you should scale, pivot, or refine.

**Phase 2: The Strategic Decision Point (End of Month 3)**

Based on the data from Phase 1, you make a strategic decision:
- **Option A:** Scale this prospect (increase budget, expand targeting, refine creative)
- **Option B:** Launch a second prospect playbook (add a new audience while maintaining the first)
- **Option C:** Pause and refine (if results aren't there, fix messaging/offers before scaling)

The key principle: Don't add complexity until you have clarity. If Phase 1 didn't work, adding more audiences won't fix it. If Phase 1 worked, you now have a proven model to replicate.

**Phase 3: Compounding Growth (Months 4-18)**

Once you have one or two proven ProfitPaths, you enter the compounding phase. You're scaling what works, layering in organic content and SEO, building brand recognition, and optimizing for repeat purchase and customer lifetime value.

This is where the exponential growth happens. But you can't skip to Phase 3. You have to earn it by proving the model first.

### **How to Know When to Scale vs. Pivot**

The decision at the end of Phase 1 is data-driven, not emotional. Here are the key metrics that determine your next move:

**Scale Indicators (Green Lights):**
- **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) < 30% of Average Order Value (AOV):** If your AOV is $500 and your CPA is under $150, you're profitable on first purchase. Scale aggressively.
- **Email-to-Purchase Conversion Rate > 3%:** If leads are converting to customers at 3%+ through your email nurture, your messaging resonates. Scale.
- **ROAS on Retargeting > 4:1:** If your retargeting campaigns are returning $4+ for every $1 spent, you have a proven conversion path. Scale.
- **Repeat Purchase Rate > 15% within 90 days:** If customers are coming back, you're building a brand, not just making transactions. Scale.

**Refine Indicators (Yellow Lights):**
- **CPA is 30-50% of AOV:** You're close to profitability but not quite there. Don't scale yet. Refine your creative, test new offers, improve landing page conversion rates.
- **High opt-in rates but low purchase rates:** Your lead magnets are working, but your email nurture or product-market fit needs work. Refine messaging.
- **Low click-through rates (CTR < 0.8%):** Your ads aren't stopping the scroll. Refine creative and hooks.

**Pivot Indicators (Red Lights):**
- **CPA > 50% of AOV with no path to improvement:** If you're spending $300 to acquire a $500 customer and you've tested multiple creative variations with no improvement, this prospect might not be viable. Pivot to a different prospect.
- **Opt-in rates < 10%:** Your lead magnet or landing page isn't compelling. Pivot your offer or messaging.
- **Email open rates < 15%:** Your subject lines or sender reputation is broken. Pivot your email strategy.

The beauty of the ProfitPath® approach is that you get these answers in 90 days, not 12 months. You're not guessing. You're making informed decisions based on real customer behavior.

## **Phase 1: Prove One Path (Months 1-3)**

Phase 1 is about one thing: proving that you can acquire customers profitably from a specific prospect type. Everything else is secondary.

Here's exactly how to execute Phase 1 for 31:33 Jewelry.

### **Pick One Prospect Playbook to Start With**

You have three complete prospect playbooks in this MAP:
1. **Prospect 1:** Established Luxury Jewelry Buyers Discovering Faith-Based Fine Jewelry
2. **Prospect 2:** Affluent Lifestyle Women (High-Net-Worth)
3. **Prospect 3:** Faith-Forward Affluent Women

**Our recommendation: Start with Prospect 1.**

Here's why:

**Prospect 1 has the highest probability of fast proof.**

This prospect already buys luxury jewelry online. She's comfortable spending $500-$2,000 on a necklace from a brand she discovered on Instagram. She understands quality markers (14k vs. 18k gold, conflict-free diamonds, hand-finishing). She doesn't need to be educated on why fine jewelry is worth the investment. She just needs to discover that 31:33 Jewelry exists and that it reflects her faith.

This means:
- **Shorter sales cycle:** She's ready to buy now, not in six months.
- **Higher AOV:** She's accustomed to luxury pricing and won't balk at $800 necklaces.
- **Lower education burden:** Your ads don't need to teach her about jewelry quality. They just need to show her that you exist and that you're for her.
- **Faster feedback loop:** You'll know within 30-45 days if your messaging resonates because she'll either buy or she won't. No long consideration period.

**Prospect 3 (Faith-Forward) might seem like the obvious choice, but it's riskier for Phase 1.**

While Prospect 3 has the strongest emotional connection to the brand message, she's also the most likely to need education about why luxury jewelry is worth the investment. She might be accustomed to $50 Christian jewelry from bookstores or Etsy. Convincing her that $800 is reasonable for a necklace takes time and trust-building.

That doesn't mean Prospect 3 isn't valuable. It means she's better suited for Phase 2, after you've proven the model with Prospect 1 and built some social proof and testimonials to leverage.

**Prospect 2 (Affluent Lifestyle Women) is the middle ground.**

She has the income and the willingness to spend, but she might not be actively searching for faith-based jewelry. Your ads need to create demand, not just capture it. This is doable, but it's a longer play than Prospect 1.

**Bottom line: Start with Prospect 1. Prove the model. Then expand to Prospects 2 and 3 in Phase 2.**

### **Launch All 3 Campaigns Simultaneously**

Within Prospect 1, you have three complete campaigns:
1. **Campaign 1:** Style Finder Quiz (Luxury Framing)
2. **Campaign 2:** Luxury Buyer's Guide
3. **Campaign 3:** Welcome Offer (Retargeting)

**Launch all three at the same time. Here's why:**

**Campaign 1 and Campaign 2 are both cold traffic acquisition campaigns, but they appeal to different mindsets.**

Campaign 1 (Style Finder Quiz) appeals to the prospect who's ready to engage but not ready to buy. She wants to explore, discover, and see what resonates with her style. The quiz is low-commitment and high-value. It's perfect for top-of-funnel engagement.

Campaign 2 (Luxury Buyer's Guide) appeals to the prospect who's in research mode. She's thinking about buying jewelry (maybe for herself, maybe as a gift) and wants to make sure she's making a smart investment. The guide positions you as an expert and builds trust before asking for the sale.

By running both campaigns simultaneously, you're casting two different nets into the same pond. Some prospects will resonate with the quiz. Others will resonate with the guide. You're not guessing which one will work better. You're testing both and letting the data tell you.

**Campaign 3 (Welcome Offer) is retargeting, and it needs to launch immediately.**

Here's the mistake most brands make: they launch cold traffic campaigns, wait to see results, and then add retargeting later. By the time they turn on retargeting, they've lost 80% of the warm traffic that engaged but didn't convert.

The right approach: turn on retargeting from day one. As soon as someone takes the quiz or downloads the guide, they enter your retargeting pool. Within hours (not days), they see your Welcome Offer ad. You're striking while the iron is hot.

This creates a seamless funnel:
1. Prospect sees your ad for the Style Finder Quiz
2. She clicks, takes the quiz, gets personalized results
3. She receives an email with her results and an introduction to 31:33 Jewelry
4. Within 24 hours, she sees a retargeting ad offering 10% off her first purchase
5. She clicks, browses the collection, and buys

Without retargeting, steps 4 and 5 don't happen. The prospect forgets about you. With retargeting, you stay top-of-mind and convert warm traffic at 3-5x higher rates than cold traffic.

**Total campaign structure for Phase 1:**
- **2 cold traffic campaigns** (Quiz + Guide) running on Google Ads and Meta Ads
- **1 retargeting campaign** (Welcome Offer) running on Google Ads and Meta Ads
- **Email nurture sequences** for quiz takers and guide downloaders
- **Abandoned cart email/SMS flows** for website visitors who don't purchase

This is a complete, integrated system. Every piece works together.

### **What to Measure**

Phase 1 is a data-gathering exercise. You're not trying to "win" in the first 90 days. You're trying to learn. Here are the metrics that matter:

**Top-of-Funnel Metrics (Cold Traffic Campaigns):**
- **Click-Through Rate (CTR):** Are your ads stopping the scroll? Target: >1% on Meta, >3% on Google Search.
- **Cost Per Click (CPC):** How much are you paying per click? Expected: $1.50-$3.00 on Meta, $2-$5 on Google.
- **Landing Page Conversion Rate:** What percentage of visitors opt in? Target: 15-30% for quiz, 20-35% for guide.
- **Cost Per Lead (CPL):** How much does it cost to get an email address? Target: <$25 for quiz, <$30 for guide.

**Mid-Funnel Metrics (Email Nurture):**
- **Email Open Rate:** Are people opening your emails? Target: >25% (luxury audiences typically have higher open rates).
- **Email Click Rate:** Are people clicking through to your website? Target: >3%.
- **Email-to-Purchase Conversion Rate:** What percentage of leads become customers? Target: >3% within 30 days.

**Bottom-of-Funnel Metrics (Retargeting & Conversion):**
- **Retargeting CTR:** Are warm prospects clicking your retargeting ads? Target: >3%.
- **Retargeting Conversion Rate:** What percentage of retargeting clicks result in purchases? Target: >4%.
- **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA):** How much does it cost to acquire a customer? Target: <$150 (assuming $500 AOV).
- **Return on Ad Spend (ROAS):** For every $1 spent, how much revenue do you generate? Target: >3:1 blended across all campaigns.

**Business Health Metrics:**
- **Average Order Value (AOV):** What's the average purchase amount? Expected: $400-$800 for Prospect 1.
- **Purchase Frequency:** Are customers buying multiple items in one order? Track items per transaction.
- **Repeat Purchase Rate:** Are customers coming back within 90 days? Target: >10% (jewelry is typically not a frequent purchase, but gifting occasions create opportunities).

**The Key Metric: Blended CPA vs. AOV**

At the end of 90 days, the most important number is your blended Cost Per Acquisition across all campaigns compared to your Average Order Value.

If your blended CPA is $120 and your AOV is $600, you're acquiring customers at 20% of their first purchase value. That's excellent. You can scale aggressively.

If your blended CPA is $200 and your AOV is $400, you're acquiring customers at 50% of their first purchase value. That's break-even or slightly unprofitable on first purchase. You'll need to rely on repeat purchases and lifetime value to be profitable. This is still viable, but it requires more capital and patience.

If your blended CPA is $300 and your AOV is $400, you're losing money on every customer. You need to refine messaging, offers, or targeting before scaling.

### **Success Criteria: What "Proof" Looks Like**

At the end of Phase 1 (90 days), you're looking for proof that this model works. Here's what proof looks like:

**Minimum Viable Proof:**
- 50+ customers acquired from Prospect 1 campaigns
- Blended CPA < 40% of AOV
- Email-to-purchase conversion rate > 2%
- ROAS on retargeting > 3:1
- At least one campaign (quiz or guide) with CPL < $30

If you hit these benchmarks, you have proof. The model works. You can scale.

**Strong Proof:**
- 100+ customers acquired
- Blended CPA < 30% of AOV
- Email-to-purchase conversion rate > 4%
- ROAS on retargeting > 4:1
- Both campaigns (quiz and guide) with CPL < $25

If you hit these benchmarks, you have strong proof. Scale aggressively and consider launching Prospect 2 in Month 4.

**Exceptional Proof:**
- 150+ customers acquired
- Blended CPA < 25% of AOV
- Email-to-purchase conversion rate > 5%
- ROAS on retargeting > 5:1
- Organic word-of-mouth and social proof building (customers posting about their purchases without prompting)

If you hit these benchmarks, you've struck gold. This is a category-defining brand in the making. Scale everything.

Here's what to expect in the first 90 days:

**Days 61-90: Proof Point**
- You have statistically significant data

- 50-100 customers acquired

## **Phase 2: The Strategic Decision Point (End of Month 3)**

At the end of 90 days, you'll sit down with your ads partner and review the data. This is the most important meeting of the year because it determines your next 6-12 months.

Here's how to make the decision.

### **Review the Data: What's Working, What's Not**

Pull a comprehensive report covering:
- Campaign-level performance (Quiz vs. Guide vs. Retargeting)
- Platform performance (Google vs. Meta)
- Ad creative performance (which hooks, images, and videos drove the most conversions)
- Audience performance (which interest stacks or keyword themes worked best)
- Email performance (which sequences converted best)
- Product performance (which pieces sold most)

Look for patterns:
- Is one campaign significantly outperforming the other?
- Is one platform (Google or Meta) driving better ROAS?
- Is one ad creative style (lifestyle vs. product-focused) resonating more?
- Are certain products (e.g., necklaces vs. bracelets) selling better?

These insights will inform your Phase 2 strategy.

### **Decision A: Optimize and Scale This Prospect**

**When to choose this option:**
- You hit "Strong Proof" or "Exceptional Proof" benchmarks
- One or both campaigns are performing well
- You have clear winners in ad creative and targeting
- ROAS is strong (>4:1 blended)

**What this looks like:**
- Increase budget by 50-100% on winning campaigns
- Expand targeting (add more interest stacks, broader keywords, lookalike audiences)
- Develop new ad creative variations based on what's working
- Test new offers (e.g., limited-edition pieces, holiday promotions)
- Double down on email/SMS nurture to increase repeat purchase rate

**Expected outcome:**
- Months 4-6: 2-3x the customer volume of Months 1-3
- Improved unit economics as algorithms optimize and you refine creative
- Building momentum toward $100K/month in revenue

### **Decision B: Launch a Second Prospect Playbook**

**When to choose this option:**
- You hit "Minimum Viable Proof" or "Strong Proof" benchmarks
- Prospect 1 is working, but you're ready to diversify
- You have budget to maintain Prospect 1 campaigns while adding Prospect 2 or 3
- You want to test whether another prospect converts better

**What this looks like:**
- Maintain current budget on Prospect 1 campaigns (don't scale yet, but don't cut either)
- Launch all 3 campaigns for Prospect 2 or Prospect 3 with 50-75% of the budget you used for Prospect 1
- Run both prospects in parallel for 60-90 days
- Compare performance and allocate future budget to the winner

**Expected outcome:**
- Months 4-6: Similar customer volume to Months 1-3, but from two different prospects
- Better understanding of which prospect is your true IMPACT Customer
- Diversified customer base (reduces risk of audience saturation)

**Which prospect to launch second:**

If Prospect 1 worked well, we recommend launching **Prospect 3 (Faith-Forward Affluent Women)** next. Here's why:

Prospect 1 and Prospect 3 are complementary, not competitive. Prospect 1 leads with luxury and reveals faith. Prospect 3 leads with faith and reveals luxury. Together, they cover the full spectrum of your white space positioning.

Prospect 2 (Affluent Lifestyle Women) is valuable but sits in the middle. She's neither as ready-to-buy as Prospect 1 nor as emotionally connected as Prospect 3. Save her for Phase 3 once you have social proof and brand recognition.

### **Decision C: Pause and Refine**

**When to choose this option:**
- You didn't hit "Minimum Viable Proof" benchmarks
- CPA is too high (>50% of AOV) with no clear path to improvement
- Opt-in rates are low (<10%) despite testing multiple lead magnets
- Email-to-purchase conversion is weak (<2%) despite testing different sequences

**What this looks like:**
- Pause paid ad spend (or reduce to minimal maintenance budget)
- Conduct customer interviews with the people who did buy (why did they buy? what almost stopped them? what would they tell their friends?)
- Refine messaging based on customer feedback
- Test new lead magnets or offers
- Improve landing page design and copy
- Relaunch in 30-60 days with updated creative and messaging

**Expected outcome:**
- Months 4-6: Minimal customer acquisition while you refine
- Avoid wasting budget on campaigns that aren't working
- Relaunch with stronger positioning and messaging in Month 5 or 6

**The hard truth:** If you hit Decision C, it doesn't mean the brand is doomed. It means your messaging or offer needs work. This is fixable. But it requires honesty and willingness to iterate.

### **The Key Principle: Don't Add Complexity Until You Have Clarity**

The biggest mistake brands make at the end of Phase 1 is adding more channels, more audiences, and more campaigns when the first set isn't working yet.

If Prospect 1 didn't work, adding Prospect 2 won't fix it. If your ads aren't converting, adding TikTok won't help. If your email nurture isn't driving sales, adding SMS won't solve it.

Fix what's broken before adding more.

Conversely, if Prospect 1 is working, don't get greedy and try to launch all three prospects at once. Scale what's working first. Then add the next prospect. Methodical growth beats chaotic expansion every time.

## **Phase 3: Compounding Growth (Months 4-18)**

Phase 3 is where the magic happens. You've proven the model. You've scaled one or two prospects. Now you're building a brand, not just running campaigns.

Here's what Phase 3 looks like:

### **Scaling What Works Across Multiple Prospects**

By Month 6 or 7, you should have at least two prospects running profitably. Your ad spend is $20K-$50K/month, and you're generating $60K-$150K/month in revenue.

At this stage:
- Continue optimizing existing campaigns (new creative, new offers, seasonal promotions)
- Launch the third prospect playbook if you haven't already
- Expand into new platforms if appropriate (Pinterest for visual discovery, YouTube for video storytelling)
- Test new campaign types (brand awareness campaigns, video view campaigns, engagement campaigns)

The goal is to maintain 3:1 to 5:1 ROAS while increasing volume. You're no longer in "prove it" mode. You're in "scale it" mode.

### **Layering in Organic Content and SEO**

Up until now, you've been focused on paid acquisition. That's correct. Paid ads give you immediate feedback and control. But in Phase 3, you start building organic channels that compound over time.

**Organic Social Media:**
- Post 3-5x per week on Instagram (mix of product, lifestyle, customer stories, founder content)
- Leverage UGC (user-generated content) by reposting customer photos and testimonials
- Build a content calendar around key themes: belonging, faith, luxury, craftsmanship, customer stories
- Engage with your community (reply to comments, DM customers, build relationships)

**SEO and Content Marketing:**
- Publish blog posts targeting long-tail keywords ("luxury Christian jewelry," "faith-based fine jewelry," "meaningful jewelry gifts")
- Create pillar pages for key topics ("The Meaning of 31:33," "How to Choose Heirloom Jewelry," "Faith-Based Gift Guide")
- Optimize product pages for search (detailed descriptions, schema markup, customer reviews)
- Build backlinks through PR, guest posts, and partnerships

**Email/SMS List Growth:**
- Your email list is your most valuable asset. By Month 12, you should have 5,000-10,000 subscribers.
- Continue running lead magnet campaigns (quiz, guides) to grow the list
- Segment your list by behavior (quiz takers, guide downloaders, purchasers, VIPs)
- Send regular newsletters (2x per month) with valuable content, not just promotions

**The compounding effect:** Paid ads drive immediate revenue. Organic content builds long-term brand equity. Together, they create a flywheel. Paid ads bring in customers. Those customers post about their purchases. Their friends see the posts and search for you. They find your organic content, join your email list, and eventually buy. The cycle repeats.

### **Building Brand Recognition and Repeat Purchase**

By Month 12, you should have 500-1,000 customers. These aren't just transactions. They're relationships.

**Focus on retention:**
- **Post-purchase email sequence:** Thank them, educate them on jewelry care, ask for a review, introduce them to other pieces.
- **VIP program:** Create a tiered loyalty program (e.g., "Belonging Circle") with perks like early access to new pieces, exclusive discounts, birthday gifts.
- **Referral program:** Incentivize customers to refer friends ("Give $50, Get $50" or "Give a gift of belonging").
- **Seasonal campaigns:** Target existing customers with gifting campaigns (Mother's Day, Christmas, birthdays, anniversaries).

**The goal:** Increase repeat purchase rate from 10-15% in Phase 1 to 25-30% by Month 18. If you can get one in four customers to buy a second piece within 12 months, your lifetime value doubles and your unit economics become incredibly strong.

### **Long-Term Customer Value Optimization**

By Month 18, you should have enough data to calculate true Customer Lifetime Value (LTV). This is the total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship with your brand.

**Example LTV calculation:**
- Average first purchase: $600
- 30% of customers make a second purchase within 12 months (average $500)
- 10% of customers make a third purchase within 24 months (average $400)
- LTV = $600 + (0.30 × $500) + (0.10 × $400) = $600 + $150 + $40 = $790

If your LTV is $790 and your CPA is $150, your LTV:CAC ratio is 5.3:1. That's exceptional. You can afford to spend up to $400 to acquire a customer and still be profitable over 24 months.

This unlocks aggressive scaling. You can outbid competitors on ads because you're playing a longer game. They're optimizing for first purchase. You're optimizing for lifetime value.

**How to increase LTV:**
- Launch complementary products (if you only sell necklaces, add bracelets and rings so customers can build a collection)
- Create limited editions and seasonal releases (give customers reasons to come back)
- Build community (host virtual or in-person events, create a private Facebook group, feature customer stories)
- Deliver exceptional customer service (make every interaction memorable, surprise and delight customers with handwritten notes or unexpected gifts)

The brands that win in luxury e-commerce aren't the ones with the best first-purchase economics. They're the ones that turn customers into lifelong advocates.

## **How to Choose the Right Agency or Ads Partner**

You have three options for executing this plan:

1. **Hire an agency** to manage everything (ads, creative, landing pages, email)
2. **Hire a freelance ads specialist** to manage just the paid ads
3. **Do it yourself** (not recommended unless you have prior experience)

For most brands at your stage, we recommend hiring a specialized agency or freelance ads specialist. Here's how to choose the right partner.

### **What to Look For in a Paid Ads Specialist**

**1. E-commerce Experience (Specifically Jewelry or Luxury Goods)**

Not all ads specialists are created equal. Someone who's great at B2B lead generation will struggle with e-commerce. Someone who's great at dropshipping won't understand luxury positioning.

Look for:
- Portfolio of e-commerce brands they've scaled (ideally jewelry, fashion, or luxury goods)
- Case studies showing ROAS and revenue growth, not just vanity metrics (impressions, reach)
- Understanding of luxury buyer psychology (long consideration periods, high AOV, importance of brand trust)

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's the highest AOV product you've successfully advertised? What was the ROAS?"
- "Have you worked with luxury or aspirational brands? How did you position them differently from mass-market brands?"
- "What's your approach to creative for high-consideration purchases?"

**2. Platform Expertise (Google Ads AND Meta Ads)**

You need someone who's excellent at both Google and Meta. Many specialists are strong in one but weak in the other.

Google Ads requires keyword research, search intent understanding, and conversion tracking. Meta Ads requires creative testing, audience building, and algorithm management. They're different skill sets.

Look for:
- Proven results on both platforms
- Ability to explain the strategic differences between Google (demand capture) and Meta (demand creation)
- Experience with retargeting and full-funnel campaigns

**Questions to ask:**
- "What percentage of your clients' budgets typically go to Google vs. Meta, and why?"
- "How do you approach creative testing on Meta? How many variations do you test per campaign?"

**3. Strategic Thinking, Not Just Execution**

You don't want a "button pusher" who just sets up campaigns and walks away. You want a strategic partner who thinks about your business holistically.

Look for:

**Questions to ask:**

- "How do you determine when to scale vs. when to pause and refine?"
- "How often will we meet to review performance, and what will those meetings cover?"

**4. Transparent Reporting and Communication**

You need real-time visibility into performance. No excuses, no hiding behind vanity metrics.

Look for:
- Weekly or bi-weekly performance reports
- Access to ad accounts (you should own the accounts, not the agency)
- Clear dashboards showing CPA, ROAS, and revenue (not just clicks and impressions)

**Questions to ask:**
- "What does your reporting look like? Can I see a sample report?"
- "Will I have direct access to the ad accounts, or do I have to go through you?"
- "How quickly do you respond when I have questions or concerns?"

### **Red Flags to Avoid**

**1. Guarantees of Specific Results**

If an agency guarantees "5x ROAS in 30 days" or "100 sales in the first month," run. No one can guarantee results in paid advertising. There are too many variables (creative quality, product-market fit, seasonality, competition).

What they can guarantee: best practices, strategic thinking, transparent reporting, and rapid iteration based on data.

**2. Long-Term Contracts with No Performance Clauses**

Some agencies lock you into 6-12 month contracts with no out. If they're not performing, you're stuck.

Look for: 90-day initial contracts with performance benchmarks. If they hit the benchmarks, you continue. If they don't, you can exit without penalty.

**3. Ownership of Ad Accounts or Creative Assets**

Some agencies create ad accounts under their own business manager and hold your creative assets hostage if you leave.

Insist on: You own the ad accounts. You own the creative. The agency has access to manage them, but if you part ways, you keep everything.

**4. Lack of E-commerce or Luxury Experience**

If their portfolio is all local service businesses or B2B SaaS, they're not the right fit for luxury jewelry e-commerce.

**5. "Spray and Pray" Approach**

If they want to launch campaigns to all three prospects on day one, or they want to add TikTok, Pinterest, and YouTube immediately, they don't understand focus.

The right partner will recommend starting with one prospect, proving it, and then scaling.
